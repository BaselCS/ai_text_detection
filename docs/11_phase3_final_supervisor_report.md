## Executive Summary

This report delivers the finalized, audited, and empirically verified findings of the **Third Phase of Experiments**, addressing all six directives and methodological queries raised by the supervisor:

1. **Second Corpus Provenance (CNN/DailyMail):** The open-weight checkpoints used to train the detector (`google/gemma-2-9b-it`, `meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct`, `mistralai/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3`, `Qwen/Qwen2-72B-Instruct`) remain publicly downloadable from Hugging Face (the API returns HTTP 200 for all four; the two Llama/Gemma repos are `gated=manual`), but every legacy hosted OpenRouter route probed is unserved: the endpoints API returns **zero providers**, including for `01-ai/yi-large` and `mistralai/mistral-7b-instruct-v0.3`. Full corpus regeneration through local/dedicated GPU hosting was skipped: producing 6,000 full-article rewrites across four 7B–72B checkpoints (especially Qwen-2-72B) is not feasible on a single consumer GPU (RTX 3060 12 GB) within the available time, and the Gemma/LLaMA repositories are gated. The supervisor's unseen-generator contingency is therefore the standard practical choice. The CNN evaluation is an **Unseen Corpus + Prompt + Generator Test**, not an isolated generator test; all six CNN columns are labelled `actual-generator (training slot)`. Under the original 7-class label space the clean result is **26.06% accuracy / 19.28% Macro-F1** (95% CI $[25.04, 27.10]$); dropping the `deepseek-v4-pro` slot and retraining a **6-class (drop-Yi-Large)** model gives **30.22% accuracy / 25.28% Macro-F1** ($[29.07, 31.40]$). A **7-class model scored over the same six classes** gives **30.34% accuracy / 25.52% Macro-F1** clean (raw **30.90% / 26.25%**), slightly *better* than the retrained model (it gets 6–7 more rows correct). The 26.06% → 30.22% shift is therefore mainly a denominator/label-space effect from removing the near-empty Yi-Large slot, not a retraining gain. The clean binary result is **84.69% accuracy / 70.27% Macro-F1**, which is **below the 87.41% majority-class baseline** (balanced accuracy **74.02%**, bootstrap 95% CI $[72.44, 75.62]$; Human recall **59.76%**, Wilson 95% CI $[56.69, 62.76]$), so it is weak cross-corpus transfer rather than strong resilience. The endpoint audit snapshot is retained at `results/phase3/T1_endpoint_audit_snapshot.txt` and regenerated by `src/phase3/task1_endpoint_audit.py`.
2. **Centroid Geometry & Feature Audit:** The feature dimensionality is exactly **20 handcrafted features** ($791$ total columns $= 768$ BERT $+ 20$ handcrafted $+ 3$ metadata), so the model's input space is **788-dimensional**. Option A applies an **XGBoost gain-weighted feature space (following the supervisor's Item 2 specification)** — a split-importance weighting, not a formal Riemannian decision metric — and places **`Gemma-2-9B`** closest to Gemma-27B paraphrases (Euclidean $0.3938$; Cosine Rank 1 at $0.3933$). The GPT-4o-mini paraphrase set is also nearest to `Gemma-2-9B` (Euclidean $0.1893$, with its own GPT-4o slot fifth at $0.4048$; Cosine $0.6118$, with GPT-4o second at $0.2439$). The centroid evidence supports, but does not “unequivocally” prove, the attractor interpretation; the classifier behaviour and Option A are the strongest evidence.
3. **RoBERTa Translation Set Audit & Remediation:** The anomalous $1.0000$ Macro-F1 was diagnosed as a compound artifact of **78.48% train/validation split leakage** and an untranslated-string pipeline bug. The historical 1,729-row file is an exact-string duplicate of articles in the full source corpus (train+val+test; 1,729/1,729 exact matches); the “1.0000 TF-IDF cosine” wording is withdrawn because the audit assigned similarity $1.0$ by construction after exact matching. Setting B1 is **99.90% ± 0.09% accuracy / 99.68% ± 0.29% Macro-F1**; Setting B2 is **99.60% ± 0.55% / 99.60% ± 0.55%**. Because B1/B2 leave Human text unperturbed while AI text carries NLLB artifacts, these near-ceiling scores are a **translation-separability ceiling**, not evidence of general robustness; NLLB artifacts contribute alongside the 72.17% balanced accuracy in Setting A.
4. **Confidence Calibration (Attractor Reduction):** Error confidence is lower in the **6-class (drop-Gemma-2-9B)** model (dropping by **29.1 pp** on Gemma-27B and **16.2 pp** on Mistral-7B as accuracy rises from **0.1340 to 0.3468** and **0.3144 to 0.4983**), although this comparison is confounded by the concurrent accuracy increase and label-space change (1/7 vs. 1/6) and therefore cannot isolate attractor removal from accuracy effects. Human-only binary accuracy is **56.16% / 53.97%** (extended/restricted), versus **96.20% / 97.15%** for AI rows; the AI-only models converge but are fitted on a near-constant outcome, so their slopes (9.80/14.95) are not read as preservation effects. The invalid independent-SE “regime invariance” test is withdrawn. A fully interacted model rejects equal BERTScore slopes across paraphrasers in the primary restricted regime (multi-class joint Wald $p = 4.23\times 10^{-25}$; binary $p = 2.33\times 10^{-19}$) and in the extended six-paraphraser sensitivity check (multi-class $p = 4.94\times 10^{-41}$; binary $p = 4.39\times 10^{-24}$), and the interaction test shows Gemma-27B has a materially lower slope than the other five paraphrasers (pooled multi-class interaction $-27.27$, clustered SE $2.34$, $p = 2.86\times 10^{-31}$). Task 6 establishes a document-level BERTScore–correctness association and paraphraser-specific slope heterogeneity; it does **not** test attractor geometry, which remains Task 2's question.

This simultaneous shift of corpus, prompt, and generator lowered 7-class attribution accuracy to $0.2656$. The supervisor directed us to check availability of original training checkpoints, drop/relabel `Yi-Large`, and if too few matched models remain, report the evaluation under the unseen-shift contingency. Because corpus, prompt, and generator all differ, we report it as an **Unseen Corpus + Prompt + Generator Test**, not an isolated unseen-generator test.

### 1.3 Methodological Rationale & Adopted Contingency
Regenerating the CNN set using current generation successors (e.g., Qwen-2.5, Gemma-3, LLaMA-3.1) would perpetuate the exact methodological confound: altering generator architectures alongside the corpus domain, thereby failing to isolate corpus shift. Local regeneration with the original weights was skipped because 6,000 full-article rewrites across four 7B–72B checkpoints (especially Qwen-2-72B) exceed the VRAM and compute time available on a single consumer GPU (RTX 3060 12 GB), and the Gemma/LLaMA repositories are gated. In that setting, the supervisor's unseen-generator contingency is the standard practical choice.

Consequently, we formally adopted the supervisor's specified contingency:
1. **Unseen Corpus + Prompt + Generator Test:** The CNN/DailyMail evaluation is reported as an out-of-distribution test in which corpus, prompt, and generator all shift simultaneously. It should not be attributed solely to novel model signatures. The three relevant comparisons are:

| Evaluation | Clean Accuracy | Clean Macro-F1 | Clean N | Raw Accuracy | Raw Macro-F1 | Raw N |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7-class closed world | 26.06% | 19.28% | 6,935 | 26.56% | 19.92% | 6,999 |
| 7-class model, six-class scoring (drop-Yi-Large) | **30.34%** | **25.52%** | 5,939 | **30.90%** | **26.25%** | 6,000 |
| 6-class (drop-Yi-Large), retrained | 30.22% | 25.28% | 5,939 | 30.80% | 26.01% | 6,000 |

The 7-class model scored over the six classes is **slightly better** than the retrained **6-class (drop-Yi-Large)** model (raw: 1,854 vs. 1,848 correct rows; clean: 1,802 vs. 1,795). Here the 7-class model's Yi-Large predictions are counted as errors; scores are not renormalised over the six logits. The apparent 26.06% → 30.22% improvement is therefore mostly a denominator/label-space effect from removing the near-empty Yi-Large slot, not a gain from retraining. All three are above 6-class or 7-class chance; the comparison remains cross-corpus and does not establish in-distribution performance.
2. **Binary Detection Transfer Is Weak:** The binary detection model achieves **84.69% clean accuracy** and **70.27% Macro-F1** (Raw: $84.66\%$ / $70.14\%$). This is **below the 87.41% majority-class baseline** on the clean split (87.50% raw), so the correct interpretation is weak cross-corpus transfer, not “substantial resilience.” Balanced accuracy is **74.02% clean** (73.96% raw; bootstrap 95% CI $[72.44, 75.62]$ and $[72.35, 75.47]$), and **Human recall is 59.76% clean** (59.70% raw; Wilson 95% CI $[56.69, 62.76]$ and $[56.63, 62.70]$); AI recall is 88.28%. The aggregate accuracy is flattered by the AI-heavy class distribution. All of these values, with intervals, are saved in `results/phase3/T1_cnn_binary_and_multiclass_ood_metrics.csv`.

## Task 2: Centroid Geometry vs. Prediction Reconciliation

### 2.1 Problem Statement & Dimensionality Audit
Preliminary isotropic Euclidean and Cosine distances computed across the 791-dimensional Feature+BERT space positioned `Yi-Large` closer to Gemma paraphrases than `Gemma-2-9B`, directly contradicting classifier predictions where Gemma paraphrases collapsed into the `Gemma-2-9B` attractor. Furthermore, the supervisor questioned whether the handcrafted feature count was 20 (as published in Paper 1) or 23 (as mentioned in preliminary Phase 2 notes).

We inspected the matrix schema of `processed_articles.csv` (**$N = 51,181$ rows**, columns $= 791$) — the authoritative corpus used by `task2_centroids.py`. * **Frozen BERT Embeddings:** 768 dimensions (`bert_0` through `bert_767`).
* **Non-Feature Metadata Columns:** Exactly 3 columns (`Writer`, `Text`, `is_AI`).
* **Handcrafted Features:** Exactly **20 features** ($791 - 768 - 3 = 20$):
  1. `ppl` (Perplexity)
  2. `burstiness`
  3. `ttr` (Type-Token Ratio)
  4. `complex` (Flesch-Kincaid / Readability Complexity)
  5. `avg_word_len`
  6. `avg_sent_len`
  7. `uid` (Uniform Information Density)
  8–17. `gain`, `beauty`, `government`, `urban`, `art`, `help`, `optimism`, `strength`, `love`, `traveling` (10 Empath lexical categories)
  18. `syntax_depth` (Syntactic parse tree depth)
  19. `semantic_mean` (Internal semantic sentence similarity mean)
  20. `semantic_std` (Internal semantic sentence similarity standard deviation)

The Phase 2 figure of 23 resulted from an inadvertent subtraction $791 - 768 = 23$, omitting to deduct the 3 metadata columns. The original paper's specification of 20 handcrafted features is verified and exact.

### 2.3 Centroid Distance in an XGBoost Gain-Weighted Feature Space
Isotropic distances across frozen BERT dimensions are dominated by semantic content rather than generative stylometrics. When distances are computed in an **XGBoost gain-weighted feature space** (the supervisor's Item 2 specification), geometric proximity largely mirrors predictions. This weighting is a split-importance heuristic, not a formal Riemannian decision metric, so the result is reported as descriptive evidence rather than as the classifier's geometry.

### 2.4 Conclusion
Gain-weighted geometry supports the attractor hypothesis, but the evidence is specification-dependent. Option A and the unweighted handcrafted Option B place Gemma-2-9B nearest to Gemma-27B paraphrases; the weighted handcrafted Option B produces a near-tie on the Gemma-2-9B paraphrase set (GPT-4o 0.6618 vs Gemma-2-9B 0.6641) and is the one specification where the GPT-4o-mini set is nearest its own slot (0.2411 vs 0.2533). The centroid analysis is retained as supporting evidence, with the near-tie disclosed; it is a descriptive gain-weighted heuristic, not a formal decision-space metric.

## Task 3: RoBERTa NLLB Translation Robustness Audit

### 3.1 Investigation of the 1.0000 Macro-F1 Anomaly
Phase 2 reported a suspicious RoBERTa binary macro-F1 of $1.0000$ on the NLLB translation set, exceeding clean test performance ($0.9978$). An audit of the historical evaluation file `data/processed/processed_translation.csv` ($N = 1,729$; 247 per class across 7 classes) uncovered two critical pipeline flaws. **Provenance clarification:** this 1,729-row file was a subsampled historical evaluation file, not the remediated test set. It suffered 78.48% train/validation leakage and retained untranslated strings. The remediated Seed-42 evaluation set ($N = 666$ after source-text deduplication) was extracted directly from the pre-subsampling NLLB pool (`archive/legacy/old_things/paraphrasing/nllb/with_semantic_similarity.csv`, $N = 3,064$) intersected with each seed's held-out test indices; the five-seed sample sizes are 666, 579, 610, 615, and 586. Only 372 of the 666 remediated rows overlap the 1,729-row audit file by `original_text` (55 Human + 317 AI), so 294 AI rows in the remediated set were not part of the leakage audit. The leakage percentages below therefore describe the historical file, not the 666-row evaluation set.
1. **Severe Train/Validation Split Leakage (78.48%):** Samples were drawn via random subsampling prior to dataset partitioning. On the primary baseline (Seed 42):
   - Training split overlap: 1,205 samples (**69.69%**)
   - Validation split overlap: 152 samples (**8.79%**)
   - True held-out test split: Only 372 samples (**21.52%**)
   - Across all five random seeds, train/val leakage was 78.48%, 81.78%, 80.74%, 79.01%, and 81.20% under the frozen first-occurrence duplicate-text mapping.
2. **Untranslated String Replacement Bug:** An exact-string audit showed that **all 1,729 texts** in `processed_translation.csv` are exact duplicates of articles in the full source corpus (train+val+test; 1,729/1,729 exact matches). The earlier “1.0000 TF-IDF cosine” wording is withdrawn: the audit did not compute TF-IDF similarity; it exact-matched strings and then assigned `match_similarity = 1.0` by construction. The data pipeline had retained unperturbed text strings rather than round-trip translated outputs. As an illustrative historical baseline on this unperturbed $N = 1,729$ set, AI articles contained structural prompt artifacts completely absent in human articles (markdown bolding `**`: **56.2%** vs. **0.0%**; `Title:` tags: **37.4%** vs. **0.0%**; `Date:` tags: **8.8%** vs. **0.0%**; bullet points: **12.7%** vs. **3.6%**). A reproducible formatting-only Random Forest (depth 4, five-fold stratified CV) reaches **86.18% accuracy / 49.77% Macro-F1** on the full 1,729 rows and **90.49% / 90.40%** on a balanced 247-Human/247-AI subset; an extended surface-feature model reaches **98.61% / 97.11%** on the full set. The earlier unsupported **94.97% / 89.71%** figure is withdrawn because its generating script and feature definition are not retained and the claimed value could not be reproduced. All primary translation robustness conclusions below rely strictly on the remediated $N = 666$ held-out test split, which has zero source-text and zero index overlap with train/val (Section 3.2 & Section 3.3).

### 3.2 Physical Shift Introduced by NLLB Translation
We retrieved the genuine NLLB-200 round-trip translation outputs from `archive/legacy/old_things/paraphrasing/nllb/with_semantic_similarity.csv` across all $N = 666$ held-out test articles for Seed 42 (`T3_surface_artifacts_comparison.csv`):
* **Text Truncation:** Average document length decreased by **-55.02%** ($2,872.44 \to 1,292.01$ characters, $p < 0.0001$; word count dropped from $444.83 \to 203.95$, -54.15%).
* **Syntactic & Punctuation Stripping:** Quotation marks dropped by **-65.48%** ($12.03 \to 4.15$), hyphens/dashes by **-62.83%** ($5.65 \to 2.10$), and markdown bullet points by **-98.82%** ($12.76\% \to 0.15\%$).
* **Surface Separability (Pre- vs. Post-NLLB on $N = 666$ Test Split):** Human test articles ($N = 55$) contain **0.0%** markdown bolding (`**`), `Title:`, `By:`, or `Date:` tags both pre- and post-translation (with bullets dropping from $7.27\% \to 0.0\%$). In AI test articles ($N = 611$), NLLB round-trip translation substantially strips surface formatting: markdown bolding drops from **57.45% $\to$ 44.35%** (-22.79%), `Title:` tags from **38.95% $\to$ 16.86%** (-56.72%), `Date:` tags from **9.49% $\to$ 5.07%** (-46.55%), and bullet points from **13.26% $\to$ 0.16%** (-98.77%).

#### 3.2.1 Remediated-Set Leakage Check and Shallow Baseline

## Task 4: Confidence Calibration on the Retrained 6-class (drop-Gemma-2-9B) Model

### 4.1 Attractor Elimination Hypothesis
In Phase 2, we showed that retraining the 7-class classifier without `Gemma-2-9B` dismantled the Gemma attractor. Task 4 evaluates whether attractor elimination also restores probabilistic calibration and resolves pathological error overconfidence.

### 4.2 Experimental Setup
We evaluated the retrained **6-class (drop-Gemma-2-9B)** model against the 7-class baseline across both the `Gemma-27B` paraphrase set ($N = 1,776$ non-Gemma scorable, $N = 2,072$ full) and the `Mistral-7B` paraphrase set ($N = 1,800$ scorable, $N = 2,100$ full). We measured:
* Mean confidence on correct vs. wrong predictions ($\mu \pm \sigma$)
* Confidence separation gap: $\Delta = \text{Conf}_{\text{Correct}} - \text{Conf}_{\text{Wrong}}$
* Expected Calibration Error (ECE, 10 equal-width bins)
* Multi-class Brier Score: $\text{Brier} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{c=1}^K (p_{ic} - y_{ic})^2 \in [0, 2]$, and Maximum Calibration Error (MCE)

### 4.4 Key Scientific Findings
1. **Pathological Overconfidence Substantially Reduced:** In the 7-class baseline, incorrect predictions exhibited extreme certainty ($\mu = 0.9039$ on Gemma-27B, $\mu = 0.8676$ on Mistral-7B) because samples were pulled blindly into the Gemma attractor. In the **6-class (drop-Gemma-2-9B)** model, error confidence drops by **29.1 percentage points** ($-32.2\%$ relative) on Gemma-27B and by **16.2 percentage points** ($-18.7\%$ relative) on Mistral-7B. This is a large relative improvement, not full elimination; 6-class (drop-Gemma-2-9B) error confidence remains 0.6129 and 0.7055.
2. **Confidence Gap Restored:** The confidence gap ($\text{Conf}_{\text{Correct}} - \text{Conf}_{\text{Wrong}}$), previously inverted ($-0.0248$) or flat ($+0.0001$), widened to a robust **$+0.1507$** and **$+0.1482$**. The classifier expresses genuine hesitation on difficult edge cases.
3. **Halving of Expected Calibration Error:** ECE decreased from $0.7666 \to \mathbf{0.3183}$ (**-58.5%**) on Gemma-27B and from $0.5538 \to \mathbf{0.2810}$ (**-49.3%**) on Mistral-7B. Brier scores improved by $43.0\%$ and $37.4\%$.

**Calibration caveat:** “Halved” is not “calibrated.” The **6-class (drop-Gemma-2-9B)** Gemma-27B Brier score is **0.8980**, which is **worse than the 0.8333 uniform-forecast Brier** (Brier skill score $-0.0776$); its ECE is still **0.3183**. On the matched 1,776-row non-Gemma scorable subset, the 7-class accuracy is **0.1340**, below the $1/7 = 0.1429$ chance rate; this qualifier matters because the full 2,072-row set scores 0.2457, above chance. The defensible claim is that removing the attractor reduces overconfidence and improves relative calibration, not that it restores good calibration. See `results/phase3/T4_calibration_benchmarks.csv`.

### 6.2 Model Specification & Cluster Adjustment

$$\text{logit}(P(Y_i = 1)) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \cdot \text{BERTScore}_i + \sum_{k=1}^5 \gamma_k \cdot \text{Paraphraser}_{i,k}$$
and a writer-fixed-effects robustness specification:
$$\text{logit}(P(Y_i = 1)) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \cdot \text{BERTScore}_i + \sum_{k=1}^5 \gamma_k \cdot \text{Paraphraser}_{i,k} + \sum_{j=1}^6 \delta_j \cdot \text{Writer}_{i,j}.$$
* **Dependent Variables $Y$:** Correct multi-class attribution (1/0) and correct binary detection (1/0). * **Missing-Row Arithmetic:** $12{,}600$ expected $= 6 \times 2{,}100$; 31 processed rows are absent (`Gemma-2-9B` $-2$, `Qwen-2-72B` $-1$, reconstructed `Gemma-27B` $-28$), and 18 mis-recovered rows are explicitly excluded. That gives $12{,}600 - 31 - 18 = 12{,}551$ retained rows, of which 12,549 have a valid BERTScore reference.
* **Robustness Regimes:**
  - **Extended-Pool Sensitivity:** all 6 paraphrasers, $N = 12{,}549$ valid rows, $G = 3{,}782$; not a same-article comparison.

Applying the six-paraphraser Bonferroni threshold ($\alpha = 0.05/6 = 0.0083$), **Gemma-27B is robustly negative** ($p = 9.48\times10^{-9}$) and **Gemma-2-9B is only nominally negative** ($p = 0.0134$, not below the corrected threshold). Higher-fidelity Gemma rewrites still collapse into the Gemma-2-9B attractor, so better semantic preservation does not rescue attribution for those families. Binary slopes remain positive for all paraphrasers.

The stacked interaction test confirms the same heterogeneity. The direct pooled interaction (Gemma-27B vs the other five) is:

| Outcome | Specification | Interaction $\beta$ | Clustered SE | $z$ | $p$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-class | Pooled interaction, no paraphraser controls | **-27.2668** | 2.3443 | -11.63 | $2.86\times 10^{-31}$ |
| Multi-class | Paraphraser controls (Gemma-27B vs GPT-4o-mini) | **-22.2764** | 2.3920 | -9.31 | $1.24\times 10^{-20}$ |
| Multi-class | Paraphraser controls + writer FE | **-33.4317** | 2.5297 | -13.22 | $7.10\times 10^{-40}$ |
| Binary | Pooled interaction, no paraphraser controls | **-16.4116** | 2.6544 | -6.18 | $6.29\times 10^{-10}$ |
| Binary | Paraphraser controls (Gemma-27B vs GPT-4o-mini) | **-17.2954** | 2.7215 | -6.36 | $2.08\times 10^{-10}$ |

The previously reported independent-SE comparison ($\Delta\beta = -4.25$, SE $1.44$, $p = 0.0032$; binary $-4.06$, SE $2.30$, $p = 0.078$) is **withdrawn**. It applied $\sqrt{SE_{\text{full}}^2 + SE_{\text{restricted}}^2}$ to nested estimates that share most of the same rows, which overstates the variance of the difference. The interaction model above is the valid test.

### 6.5 Key Empirical Insights
1. **Positive association, not causal proof:** Every pooled specification shows a positive BERTScore–correctness association with $p < 10^{-7}$. For the multi-class outcome, every non-Gemma slope is positive (with GPT-4o-mini the weakest positive slope, $p = 0.014$), while the two Gemma slopes are negative; binary slopes remain positive for all six paraphrasers. This is stronger than six aggregate points, but it remains observational: BERTScore covaries with prompt difficulty, refusal/truncation, and writer identity.
2. **Multi-class effect is real but modest:** Baseline ORs are **2.09** (extended) and **2.64** (restricted) per $+0.10$ BERTScore, with pseudo $R^2$ only 0.065/0.063. Writer FE raises the OR to 23.45/25.01 but also raises pseudo $R^2$ to 0.38/0.36, showing that writer/class identity explains much of the outcome.
3. **The binary ORs are confounded by class composition:** Raw baseline ORs of **36.04** and **47.53** collapse to **6.59** and **6.50** with writer FE. Human rows have lower mean BERTScore and only **56.16%** (extended) / **53.97%** (restricted) binary accuracy, versus **96.20%** / **97.15%** for AI rows. The pooled binary model is partly separating Human from AI rather than measuring semantic preservation. Human-only ORs are **3.36/3.76**; the AI-only models converge but their outcome is near-constant, so their slopes (9.80/14.95) are not interpreted as preservation effects. The defensible binary claim is the Human-only range of roughly **3.4–3.8×**, not 36–48×.
4. **Slope heterogeneity is the robust finding:** The common-slope restriction is rejected in the **primary restricted 5-paraphraser regime** (multi-class joint Wald $p = 4.23\times 10^{-25}$; binary $p = 2.33\times 10^{-19}$) and in the **extended 6-paraphraser sensitivity regime** that adds Gemma-27B (multi-class $p = 4.94\times 10^{-41}$; binary $p = 4.39\times 10^{-24}$). Gemma-27B is robustly negative (Bonferroni $\alpha = 0.0083$), Gemma-2-9B is nominally negative ($p = 0.0134$), and GPT-4o-mini, LLaMA-3, Mistral-7B, and Qwen-2-72B have positive slopes. This is consistent with the attractor interpretation but is not itself an attractor-geometry test.
5. **Task 6 does not test Task 2's attractor claim:** Task 6 estimates document-level BERTScore–correctness associations and paraphraser-specific slope heterogeneity. It does not measure centroid geometry, feature-space collapse, or causal attribution. The earlier sentence “the core narrative is supported by a corrected document-level analysis” is therefore replaced by the narrower claim that semantic preservation is a consistent, heterogeneous correlate of detector retention.

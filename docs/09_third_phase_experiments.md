### 2. Centroid Geometry vs. Prediction Conflict
- **Problem**:
  - Unweighted Euclidean/Cosine distances in the 791-column Features+BERT space (788 model features) placed `Yi-Large` nearest to Gemma paraphrases, not `Gemma-2-9B`. The geometric analysis contradicted the classifier predictions.
- **Action Required**:
  - Recompute distances in the space the model actually uses:
    - **Option A**: Weighted by XGBoost feature gain.
    - **Option B**: Restricted to handcrafted features only.
  - Confirm feature count: verify why Phase-2 reported 23 features whereas the first paper reported 20 features.
  - *Note*: If the recomputed geometry still does not match predictions, drop the centroid analysis entirely rather than publish a contradiction.
- **Resolution & Status (DONE)**:
  > **Task 2: Resolution of Feature Count & Gain-Weighted Centroid Geometry**
  > 
  > 1. **Feature Count Clarification:** The authoritative dataset (`data/processed/processed_articles.csv`) contains **$N = 51,181$ rows** and 791 total columns: 768 BERT embeddings, 3 metadata columns (`Writer`, `Text`, `is_AI`), and exactly **20 handcrafted features** ($791 - 768 - 3 = 20$). `task2_centroids.py` computes on all 51,181 rows and loads the 788 model features from `results/models/Multi_BERT_metadata.json` (`feature_list`), asserting equality with `results/models/Bin_BERT_features.json` and raising if any column is absent from a paraphrase file (no zero-fill). The GPT-4o-mini feature file now contains all 791 columns: `uid` and the 10 Empath categories were regenerated with the original `helper/paraphrase_feature_eval.py` pipeline rather than imputed. The confusion in the preliminary report arose from taking $791 - 768 = 23$ without deducting the 3 non-feature metadata fields. The first paper's count of 20 features is verified and exact.
  > 
  > 2. **Geometric Alignment with Predictions:** The previous contradiction was primarily an artifact of isotropic, unweighted distances across the frozen 768-dimensional BERT space. Recomputing centroid distances in **an XGBoost gain-weighted feature space (following the supervisor's Item 2 specification)** largely resolves it. This is a split-importance weighting, not a formal Riemannian decision metric: tree gain is a split-quality statistic, comparable only within one fitted model. The paraphrase sets entering the centroids are $N = 2{,}072$ (Gemma-27B), $2{,}098$ (Gemma-2-9B), $2{,}099$ (Qwen-2), $2{,}100$ (Mistral-7B), $2{,}100$ (LLaMA-3), and $2{,}100$ (GPT-4o-mini):
  >    - **Option A (XGBoost Gain-Weighted, 788-d):** The nearest centroid to Gemma paraphrases is **`Gemma-2-9B`** (Euclidean: $0.3938$ vs. $0.4521$ for `Yi-Large`; Cosine: Rank 1 at $0.3933$ vs. $0.1092$ for `Mistral-7B` and $0.1023$ for `Yi-Large`). Qwen-2 and Mistral-7B also converge toward Gemma-2-9B, and the GPT-4o-mini set now resolves to Gemma-2-9B as its nearest centroid ($0.1893$ Euclidean, with its own GPT-4o slot fifth at $0.4048$; Cosine $0.6118$, with GPT-4o second at $0.2439$), whereas the earlier 780-column file had spuriously placed it on Qwen-2-72B.
  >    - **Option B (20 Handcrafted Features Only):** On the Gemma-27B paraphrase set, **`Gemma-2-9B`** is closest under Euclidean ($1.7296$ vs. $1.8016$ for `GPT-4o` and $2.7508$ for `Yi-Large`) and Cosine ($0.8081$, Rank 1 vs. $0.6906$ for `GPT-4o` and $-0.0920$ for `Yi-Large`).
  >    - **Sensitivity (must be disclosed):** The result is not invariant to every handcrafted reweighting. On the **Gemma-2-9B paraphrase set**, the unweighted `OptionB_Handcrafted` Euclidean distance is Gemma-2-9B $1.9630$ vs. GPT-4o $1.9754$ (0.6% margin in favour of Gemma), but the `OptionB_GainWeighted` Euclidean variant gives GPT-4o $0.6618$ vs. Gemma-2-9B $0.6641$ (0.35% margin in favour of GPT-4o). The GPT-4o-mini set is the one case where the gain-weighted handcrafted variant is closest to its own slot (GPT-4o $0.2411$ vs. Gemma-2-9B $0.2533$). The earlier word “unequivocally” is withdrawn. The primary evidence remains Option A plus classifier behaviour.
  > 
  > *Conclusion:* Gain-weighted geometry supports the attractor hypothesis, but the evidence is specification-dependent. The analysis is retained with the near-tie disclosed, not presented as an unconditional geometric proof.

- **Resolution & Status (DONE)**:
  > **Task 3: Resolution & Audit of RoBERTa Translation Set Anomaly (1.0000 F1)**
  > 
  > Comprehensive programmatic audit of the historical evaluation file `data/processed/processed_translation.csv` (1,729 samples: 247 per class × 7 classes) resolved the root cause behind RoBERTa's anomalous 1.0000 F1 score. **Provenance:** this 1,729-row file was a subsampled historical evaluation file, not the remediated test set. The remediated Seed-42 set ($N=666$ after source-text deduplication; five-seed $N=666, 579, 610, 615, 586$) was extracted directly from the pre-subsampling NLLB pool (`with_semantic_similarity.csv`, $N=3,064$) intersected with each seed's held-out test indices. Only 372/666 remediated rows overlap the 1,729-row audit file by `original_text` (55 Human + 317 AI); 294 AI rows were not audited. The leakage percentages below therefore describe the historical file.
  > 
  > 1. **Severe Train-Set Split Leakage (78.48% Leaked):**
  >    - Matching the 1,729 samples back to the full source corpus (`processed_articles.csv`, $N = 51{,}181$ rows spanning train, val, and test) reveals that they were **not** drawn exclusively from the test split.
  >    - For the primary baseline (**Seed 42**):
  >      - **In-Train Count:** 1,205 samples (**69.69%**)
  >      - **In-Val Count:** 152 samples (**8.79%**)
  >      - **In-Test Count:** Only 372 samples (**21.52%**)
  >      - **Total Leakage into Training/Val:** **78.48%** (1,357 of 1,729 texts had been seen during model training/tuning).
  >    - This leakage is consistent across all seeds under the frozen first-occurrence duplicate-text mapping (Seed 42: 78.48%, Seed 123: 81.78%, Seed 999: 80.74%, Seed 2024: 79.01%, Seed 8080: 81.20%). The 1,729-row file is constant across seeds; what varies is which split its source articles fell into, so the held-out slice ranges from 315 to 372 rows (18.22%–21.52%). The mapping rule is fixed in `src/phase3/task3_leakage_audit.py` and summarised in `T3_leakage_audit_summary.csv`.
  > 
  > 2. **Untranslated Text Pipeline Discrepancy:**
  >    - An **exact-string audit** shows that all 1,729 texts in `processed_translation.csv` are exact duplicates of articles in the full source corpus (1,729/1,729 exact matches against train+val+test). The earlier phrase “1.0000 TF-IDF cosine” is withdrawn: the audit did not compute TF-IDF similarity; it exact-matched strings and then assigned `match_similarity = 1.0` by construction.
  >    - In the data engineering pipeline, the `Text` column in `processed_translation.csv` retained the unperturbed original text rather than the round-trip translated text (which resided in an intermediate column).
  >    - Consequently, RoBERTa was evaluating largely memorized training samples (~78.5% of the evaluation set), directly driving the 1.0000 F1 score on Seed 42.
  >    - On the **deduplicated 666-row test set**, the split check is clean: **0 source-text overlaps** with train/val and **0 index overlaps** with train/val, in addition to **0 exact string matches** to the full 51,181-row source corpus. Reproduce with `src/phase3/task3_667_leakage_audit.py`; summary in `results/phase3/T3_667_leakage_audit.json`.

> 6. **Formal Exclusion Confirmation — Claude-3-Haiku:**
>    - **Methodological Statement for Manuscript:** Claude-3-Haiku was successfully generated across all 1,000 CNN-DailyMail prompts (`claude_haiku` column) and was retained for the zero-shot Binary Detection evaluation (where all AI generators map to $\text{is\_AI} = 1$). However, Claude-3-Haiku was intentionally and formally excluded from the Multi-Class Attribution evaluation.
>    - **Technical Rationale:** The Phase-1 hybrid multi-class classifier was trained under a closed-world label set of seven classes (`GPT-4o`, `Gemma-2-9B`, `Human`, `Llama-8B`, `Mistral-7B`, `Qwen-2-72B`, `Yi-Large`). > ### Key Statistical Insights:
> 1. **Positive association, not causal proof:** all pooled specifications are significant ($p < 10^{-7}$). For the multi-class outcome, every non-Gemma slope is positive (with GPT-4o-mini the weakest positive slope, $p=0.014$), while the two Gemma slopes are negative; binary slopes remain positive for all six paraphrasers. The design is observational; BERTScore covaries with prompt difficulty, refusals, and writer identity.
> 2. **Multi-class effect is modest:** baseline ORs are 2.09 (extended) and 2.64 (restricted), pseudo $R^2$ 0.065/0.063. Writer FE raises the OR to 23.45/25.01 and pseudo $R^2$ to 0.38/0.36.
> 3. **Binary ORs are confounded by class composition:** baseline ORs are 36.04/47.53 and collapse to 6.59/6.50 with writer FE. Human rows have lower BERTScore and only 56.16% (extended) / 53.97% (restricted) binary accuracy versus 96.2% / 97.15% for AI. The defensible claim is the Human-only range of roughly 3.4–3.8×, not 36–48×; AI-only models converge but their slope is estimated on a near-constant outcome and is not a preservation effect.
> 4. **Slope heterogeneity is the robust finding:** equal slopes are rejected in both the primary restricted 5-paraphraser regime (multi-class joint Wald $p=4.23\times10^{-25}$; binary $p=2.33\times10^{-19}$) and the extended 6-paraphraser sensitivity regime that adds Gemma-27B (multi-class $p=4.94\times10^{-41}$; binary $p=4.39\times10^{-24}$). The **Gemma-family** paraphrasers drive the multi-class result: Gemma-27B is robustly negative ($p=9.48\times10^{-9}$, below Bonferroni $\alpha=0.0083$), Gemma-2-9B is nominally negative ($p=0.0134$, not below that threshold), and the other four are positive. This is consistent with, but does not itself test, the Task 2 attractor geometry.
> 5. **Task separation:** Task 6 establishes a document-level BERTScore–correctness association and paraphraser-specific slope heterogeneity. It does **not** measure centroid geometry, feature-space collapse, or causal attribution. “Regime invariance” and “completely independent of Gemma-27B” are withdrawn.
